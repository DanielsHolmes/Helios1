Historically, commercial banks financed ground-up infill through depository relationships. However, heightened regulatory reserve requirements have compelled regional lenders to retrench. Sponsors are increasingly turning to dedicated private credit funds that understand construction mechanics, hold interest reserves, and close within weeks rather than months.

2. Sizing Interest Reserves: The 18-to-24 Month Horizon

Underwriters analyze whether interest reserves are financed within the facility or funded out-of-pocket by the sponsor. Sizing should reflect conservative completion timelines:

  • Dutch Interest vs. As-Drawn Interest: Ensuring the borrower only pays on drawn capital rather than full commitment amount from day one.
  • Weather and Permitting Cushion: Factoring 60 to 90 additional days beyond the general contractor's baseline delivery schedule.
  • Contingency Replenishment Triggers: Clear mechanics for when interest reserve balances require sponsor true-ups.

4. Contingency Allocation: Hard Cost vs Soft Cost Protections

  1. Hard Cost Contingency (5% to 10%): Dedicated exclusively to unexpected material inflation, subcontractor replacements, and structural change orders.
  2. Soft Cost Buffer (3% to 5%): Accounting for extended architecture, engineering, insurance premiums, and municipal inspection retainage.
  3. Developer Fee Subordination: Structuring sponsor profit distributions upon Certificate of Occupancy (C.O.) rather than ongoing overhead draws.

Every construction loan is only as strong as its exit strategy. In residential developments, lenders assess whether the asset will be liquidated through individual unit sales or stabilized into a long-term DSCR rental portfolio loan.

verified Key Takeaways for Developers & Sponsors
